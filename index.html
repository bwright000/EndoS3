EndoS3: Endoscopic Semantic Splatting SLAM

abstract

Purpose. Despite advances in 3D surgical scene understanding, semantic simultaneous localisation and mapping (SLAM) remains under-explored for two key reasons. First, it requires solving three challenging tasks in parallel: camera pose tracking, high-fidelity deformable reconstruction, and semantic mapping, making progress particularly demanding. Second, progress is further limited by the scarcity of datasets that provide the ground truth needed to evaluate all three tasks comprehensively under procedural conditions that closely reflect real endoscopic environments.

We propose EndoS3, a semantic SLAM framework based on 3D Gaussian splatting that uses per-frame instance masks to guide all three tasks. An instance-level motion decomposition separates static from dynamic instances, testing the flow that camera motion cannot explain against both flow noise and neighbouring instances. The camera is tracked against the static Gaussians, while an instance-aware warp field gives each dynamic instance its own control nodes and deformation network. A semantic node allocation redistributes a fixed node budget across instances according to their size and non-rigidity. We also introduce CRCD++, an ex-vivo cholecystectomy dataset with RGB-D frames, camera poses and instance annotations for full semantic SLAM evaluation.

Results. We compare EndoS3 with state-of-the-art general-purpose and surgical semantic SLAM methods in tracking, reconstruction and semantic mapping on the CRCD++ and the SemSup datasets. EndoS3 surpasses all previous approaches.

Conclusion. Modelling motion and deformation with semantic information supports accurate semantic SLAM in deformable surgical scenes. EndoS3 and CRCD++ provide a method and a benchmark for improved geometric and semantic understanding in endoscopic environments.

method

Instance-level motion decomposition

Optical flow from WAFT, minus the flow explained by a PnP camera estimate, is pooled in superpixels split along instance boundaries. Each instance is tested against flow noise and against its neighbours; only instances that pass both are dynamic. The camera is tracked against the static part.

Instance-aware warp field

Every instance gets its own control nodes and deformation network, and motion only propagates within an instance, so instruments and tissue no longer blend at their contact points.

Semantic node allocation

A fixed node budget is redistributed across instances every frame, weighted by visible area and non-rigidity, so nodes follow instruments entering the view and regions that deform most.

crcd++_dataset

20 ex-vivo robotic cholecystectomy sequences (200–2,108 frames; 14,688 stereo pairs) built on eCRCD, with dense human-annotated masks for background, liver, gallbladder and tools on every frame (one instance ID per tool), camera poses, and FoundationStereo depth.
